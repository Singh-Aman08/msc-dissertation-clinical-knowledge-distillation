# Quality-Aware Knowledge Distillation of Large Language Models for Efficient Personalised Clinical Report Generation

**MSc Data Science Dissertation — University of Bristol, 2026**

**Author:** Aman Kumar Singh  
**Supervisor:** [Dr Edwin Simpson](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/people/person/Edwin-Simpson-8ec09f42-639a-4b57-9c5b-1708724e9c7a/)

## Overview

Neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs) are highly heterogeneous, with substantial variation in clinical presentation even among individuals with the same diagnosis. In rare genetic NDDs, this can make it difficult for parents to understand their child's condition and communicate their needs to healthcare professionals. Personalised, parent-held clinical reports could help bridge this gap by combining individual patient findings with condition-specific knowledge, but producing such reports manually requires considerable clinical expertise and time.

Large language models (LLMs) offer a potential means of automating this process by integrating patient-specific information with clinical knowledge. However, clinical report generation requires more than fluent text: reports must be factually reliable, sufficiently comprehensive, concise, and appropriately communicated. LLMs can also be computationally expensive and may introduce hallucinations, omissions, or inconsistencies, creating challenges for deployment in privacy-sensitive clinical settings.

This project investigates **knowledge distillation** as a means of transferring report-generation capabilities from a large teacher language model to a substantially smaller and more efficient student model. The central premise is that the quality of the supervision provided by the teacher matters: teacher-generated reports can vary in factuality, information coverage, conciseness, and communication quality, yet conventional response-based distillation may treat them as equally valuable.

To address this, the project proposes a **multidimensional quality-aware response-based distillation framework** in which multiple dimensions of teacher-response quality determine the contribution of individual training examples. This allows useful but imperfect responses to be retained while reducing the influence of lower-quality supervision, rather than relying solely on a single quality criterion or a binary retain-or-discard decision.

The approach is investigated using **KBG syndrome**, a rare genetic neurodevelopmental disorder, as a controlled clinical case study. A synthetic data-generation pipeline produces diverse patient profiles and doctor–parent consultations, which are combined with KBG syndrome knowledge to generate personalised reports using a large teacher model. These reports are then used to train a substantially smaller student model.

The resulting models are evaluated against an **untrained baseline, conventional supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and Direct Preference Optimisation (DPO)**. A multidimensional evaluation framework assesses report quality across factuality, information coverage, conciseness, and communication quality, alongside complementary lexical and semantic measures. The study also evaluates the practical efficiency of the compact model through **model size, memory consumption, throughput, and latency**.

## Research Question

> Can multidimensional quality-aware knowledge distillation improve the clinical quality of reports generated by compact language models compared with conventional supervised fine-tuning?
