The child has large front teeth, which may be a characteristic feature of KBG syndrome.

b) How this affects others with the syndrome:
Individuals with KBG syndrome may experience dental issues, including weak enamel and dental anomalies.

## 10) EDUCATION

a) How this affects the patient:
The child's development has been closely monitored, and her educational needs have been addressed through her Educational Health Care Plan (EHCP).

b) How this affects others with the syndrome:
Individuals with KBG syndrome may require extra support in school, and some may benefit from special schooling or adaptations.

## 11) BEHAVIOUR AND DEVELOPMENT

a) How this affects the patient:
The child experiences coordination challenges and may have difficulties with physical activities or sports.

b) How this affects others with the syndrome:
Individuals with KBG syndrome may experience behavioral differences, including autism spectrum disorder (ASD), ADHD, and anxiety.

## 12) SKELETAL

a) How this affects the patient:
N/A

b) How this affects others with the syndrome:
Individuals with KBG syndrome may experience skeletal anomalies, including short fingers (brachydactyly) and curved 5th fingers (clinodactyly).

## 13) RECOMMENDATIONS FOR SCREENING AND TREATMENTS

* Regular dental check-ups
* Regular hearing reviews to age 5 (and earlier if necessary)
* Eyesight (ophthalmology) review
* Check position of testes in boys
* Consider a palate review (particularly if there are feeding difficulties or speech concerns)
* Referral for a cardiac review (including echo and ECG) following diagnosis
* Consider a skeletal review (X-ray of the wrist, hip, spine, and skull) in children following diagnosis
* Monitor growth velocity: if height is below the 2nd centile, consider referral for endocrine investigations on an individual basis
* Physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy, and behavioral therapy may be beneficial for the child.
